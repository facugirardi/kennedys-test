# Boutique, editorial and branded-residence luxury real estate sites: a design teardown for Kennedy's (kennedys.group) sales section

Research date: 2026-10-20. Method: live fetches of homepages, a text-only fetch tool (no screenshots, so it can't judge visual styling, motion or exact typography unless a secondary source names them), plus design-press searches. Access limits: fantasticfrank.com returned HTTP 429 twice, lucasfox.com and fourseasons.com/residences returned 403, sixsensesresidences.com refused the connection, and Kretz/Coldwell Banker Côte d'Azur and Marbella boutiques were not reached within the tool budget. Those sites are covered only by secondary sources or listed as gaps.

## How the sites tell a story (editorial copy, long-form narrative, journal, architecture-first presentation, photography)

### Takeaway
The clearest editorial model is The Modern House (TMH). Architecture is the subject: there's an architecture journal, an architect directory, researched property histories with archive images, and commissioned photography that can be viewed full screen. The homepage leads with a journal piece ("Today at The Modern House"), not a listing. Fantastic Frank tells its story through art direction and interior styling ("Inspire to Buy"). Branded residences (Aman) sell immersion in a destination and a lifestyle, not square metres. Greek and Riviera competitors mostly use the vocabulary of luxury ("elegance, exclusivity, excellence") without the editorial substance.

### Cited Findings
- TMH homepage hero is a full-width interior photo labeled "Today at the Modern House", featuring a journal article ("The Designer's Toolbox: THING") with "Read more". Listings come after it. — [The Modern House](https://www.themodernhouse.com/)
- TMH title tag reads "Selling the UK's most inspiring living spaces". The homepage weaves in press quotes as social proof: Evening Standard ("An estate agency like no other…"), GQ ("One of the 100 Best Things in the World"), and Gentleman's Journal. — [The Modern House](https://www.themodernhouse.com/)
- The 2015 TMH relaunch (by Field Projects, for the 10th anniversary) launched an "online architecture journal", a "directory of architects and designers", sales particulars with "information about the architects", "a carefully researched history section, including archive imagery", photography "given greater prominence and can be viewed in full screen", and "subtle, short animations… in selected sections to accentuate the visual content". — [TMH journal: relaunch](https://www.themodernhouse.com/journal/what-were-seeing-the-modern-house-relaunch); [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/inspiration/the-modern-house-identity-by-field-projects/)
- The Field Projects design "takes its cue from some of the country's finest Modern architecture, as well as from the print works of artists such as Richard Serra and Ben Nicholson", with original architectural photography commissioned for the project. — [TMH journal](https://www.themodernhouse.com/journal/what-were-seeing-the-modern-house-relaunch)
- In 2020, StudioSmall refreshed the TMH identity. Söhne (Klim) became the primary typeface and wordmark, replacing Gill Sans, with Adobe Caslon as the editorial serif companion. The palette uses "earthy colours" (black, brown, grey, white) that evoke building materials. A flexible "holding system" around the wordmark generates sub-brands (T Magazine H, Times M H, Talk M H). Print is on uncoated Fedrigoni stock. Photography credit: Elliott Sheppard. Illustration: Lucia Vinti and Marcus Oakley. Designer quote (Darcy Ward): the identity "already worked quite well" and "mainly required taking stock… and revitalising it". — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/feed/studiosmall-prepare-modern-house-modern-world-elegant-identity-system/)
- TMH extends the brand into print (a magazine) and talks. — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/feed/studiosmall-prepare-modern-house-modern-world-elegant-identity-system/)
- Fantastic Frank (founded in Stockholm in 2010, offices across Europe, Lisbon in 2021, a US partnership with SLATE in Denver) employs art directors, interior stylists, and architectural and fashion photographers. Its motto is "Inspire to Buy". Browsing its listings is described as "more akin to perusing a design publication, where curated imagery is paired with thoughtful narrative", and its properties are often picked up by design magazines. — [Modern in Denver](https://modernindenver.com/fantasticfrank/); [The Portugal News](https://www.theportugalnews.com/news/2021-04-09/fantastic-frank-comes-to-lisbon/59202)
- Fantastic Frank extends the brand into physical space: its Berlin showroom (Note Design Studio, 2021) reflects "their philosophy and values". The firm says it has "always been collaborating closely with interior designers, architects, painters, photographers". — [Note Design Studio](https://notedesignstudio.se/index/fantastic-frank-berlin)
- Aman Residences intro copy reads "Permanent immersion into the Aman lifestyle." and "To own an Aman Residence is to see a destination in a new light." Its storytelling is location-led, with large destination imagery and references to local heritage and architects. — [Aman Residences](https://www.aman.com/residences)
- BARNES runs a lifestyle grid, "The French art of living" (interior design, art, cars, yachting, concierge, fine wines, fine violins), and a print magazine (No. 40, "Celebrating La Dolce Vita"). It positions itself as an ambassador of French art de vivre. — [BARNES](https://www.barnes-international.com/en)
- Greece Sotheby's runs a blog with market-intelligence headlines (e.g. "Greece's Double Credit Milestone and the Confidence Behind Luxury Demand"), an "Intelligence Hub", and a Press section (e.g. Robb Report on a $7.8M Athens Riviera maisonette). Its listings carry evocative names (Pan's Melody, Maia, Cyllene). The copy leans on the words "elegance, exclusivity, and excellence". — [Greece Sotheby's](https://www.sothebysrealty.gr/)
- Ploumis Sotiropoulos (Christie's affiliate) uses the headlines "Exclusive Real Estate in Greece" and "We are where luxury lives", plus a long SEO block titled "Invest in a Unique Property in Greece!". Its homepage has no editorial feed: there's a "Magazine" menu item, and the press cards link off-site. — [Ploumis Sotiropoulos](https://www.ploumis-sotiropoulos.gr/en)

### Inferences
- What sets the editorial leaders apart is that the content is about something (architecture, architects, history, design culture), not about luxury itself. Kennedy's equivalent subjects include Cycladic vernacular architecture, the island architects, stone, light and wind, the history of each plot, and the people who make the islands (makers, chefs, captains). Its rental and concierge business already knows these well.
- Property naming (TMH: "The Sunday School", "The Bridge House"; Sotheby's GR: "Cyllene") is a cheap, high-impact storytelling device. Named houses read as editorial subjects. Reference codes read as inventory.
- Kennedy's has something no pure sales agency has: real stays and guest experiences in its rental villas. A "Live it before you own it" narrative (rent the villa, then buy) is an editorial and commercial bridge that Aman-style branded residences only imply.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch the current Fantastic Frank site (HTTP 429), so its current layout, typography and listing copy are unverified.
- I couldn't fetch the Six Senses Residences (connection refused) or Four Seasons Private Residences (403) pages, so their storytelling isn't documented here.
- I found no source describing TMH's current long-form property copy word for word. No detail page was fetchable because the sales list renders client-side and returned 0 results.

## Home/landing structure, hero, navigation, typography, palette, whitespace, grid

### Takeaway
The boutique leaders keep navigation short and curatorial. TMH's top-level nav names collections ("Prime / New / Featured / Past Sales"), not a search form, and the homepage is sequenced like a magazine. Big networks (BARNES, Beauchamp, Greece Sotheby's, Ploumis) open with or quickly reach a multi-field search panel and mega-menus, then an SEO-heavy footer. That reads as portal, not publication.

### Cited Findings
- TMH homepage order: journal hero, then Prime (4 listings + "View All Prime"), New, Featured (with a GQ quote and a "How much is my home worth?" CTA), Past Sales (an Evening Standard quote and "Get a free market appraisal"), then Press/About and a "Sold" strip. Nav: Prime, New, Featured, Past Sales, About, Sell. — [The Modern House](https://www.themodernhouse.com/)
- TMH typography: Söhne (grotesk, wordmark and UI) plus Caslon (editorial serif). Palette: earthy black, brown, grey and white. — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/feed/studiosmall-prepare-modern-house-modern-world-elegant-identity-system/)
- Aman nav: a logo, a 7-language switcher, a persistent "Enquire" button, and a destination mega-menu grouped by region with status tags. The hero is a "New & Noteworthy" feature (Amanvari, Mexico, "Now open"). — [Aman Residences](https://www.aman.com/residences)
- BARNES hero is a rotating slideshow of four listings with factual captions ("A 288 m² renovated house with 6 bedrooms. Notting Hill…"), followed by a Buy/Rent/Seasonal search module. The utility bar has 5 languages, a currency selector (including Bitcoin), an m²/sqft toggle, favourites and login. The footer is a large SEO directory. The page states its country count inconsistently (22 vs "over 19"). — [BARNES](https://www.barnes-international.com/en)
- Beauchamp (Riviera site) header carries a phone, an email, four office links, EN/FR and "GET IN TOUCH", with a sticky nav on scroll. The mega-menus are BUY, RENT, SERVICES, OWNER, FEES, AGENCIES, NEWS and CONTACT. The homepage opens with a headline and intro paragraph (no hero banner confirmed), then a filter panel (status, type, area, city, district, beds, price) and office cards. It mixes sales, holiday rentals and Cannes Congress rentals on one homepage. Offices: Cannes, Mougins, Saint-Tropez, Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat, Marbella, Mykonos and Tel Aviv. — [Beauchamp](https://www.beauchamp.com/)
- Greece Sotheby's hero is the tagline "Greece's Leading Luxury Real Estate Brokerage", with a development slide ("Exceptional. Contemporary. Panoramic."). Nav: Buy, Sell, Private, Rentals, Intelligence Hub, Blog, Press, About, Contact. The search filters type, area and price (up to €1M/2M/5M/10M). Curated collection tiles: Waterfront Villas, Paros Villas, Mykonos Villas, Corfu Villas, Athens Riviera Homes, Athens Centre Homes. — [Greece Sotheby's](https://www.sothebysrealty.gr/)
- Ploumis Sotiropoulos shows a duplicated nav, a dense search panel (Buy/Rent, Residential/Commercial, area, category, price, code, interior and land size, results per page), stacked CTA blocks, a stats band ("A LEGACY OF EXCELLENCE SINCE 1924": 1,400+ transactions, 100+ years, 50+ destinations), an FAQ accordion, placeholder "#" links, and inconsistent brand-name spelling. — [Ploumis Sotiropoulos](https://www.ploumis-sotiropoulos.gr/en)

### Inferences
- A pairing of grotesk and classic serif (the Söhne + Caslon model) is the reference for an editorial-luxury voice. For Kennedy's this suggests a refined serif for display and narrative text, a quiet grotesk for data and UI, and a mineral palette drawn from the islands (whitewash, Cycladic stone, sand, olive, deep Aegean ink) rather than gold accents.
- Name sections as collections ("Cyclades", "Athens Riviera", "Côte d'Azur", "Andalucía", plus "Private / Off-market", "Recently sold") instead of opening with a search bar. With small inventory, a search panel exposes how thin the stock is. A curated sequence makes the same stock feel selective.

### Gaps
- Exact grid systems, whitespace, font sizes and the colour values of live sites weren't observable. The text-only fetch can't render pages, so a visual audit (screenshots) is needed.

## Listing pages: presenting a small curated inventory (minimal filters, editorial grid, status labels)

### Takeaway
TMH treats status and provenance as content. "Sold" listings and a "Past Sales" collection stay visible as proof of taste, "Open Home: [date]" labels create events, tenure is shown, and the 2015 relaunch moved to a single scrollable list instead of pagination. Aman uses lifecycle labels ("Now open", "Coming soon", "Opening 2028/2030", "Waitlist", "Resale") with a single region filter.

### Cited Findings
- TMH cards show name, price, location and tenure (Freehold, Leasehold, Share of Freehold). Labels include "Open Home: [date]", "Sold" and "New". No "under offer" label was observed on the homepage. — [The Modern House](https://www.themodernhouse.com/)
- The TMH sales list offers filters for Price, Location, Type, Bedrooms and Status, plus "Prime" and "Inigo" (Inigo is TMH's sister brand for historic homes), and an "Order by" control. — [TMH sales list](https://www.themodernhouse.com/sales-list/homes/all/)
- TMH 2015: "the sales properties available as a scrollable list rather than spread over several pages." — [TMH journal](https://www.themodernhouse.com/journal/what-were-seeing-the-modern-house-relaunch)
- Aman has a single region filter (Any / Americas & Caribbean / EMEA / Asia). Card: image, "Aman Residences, [Property]", location, blurb, "Discover more". Status shows in the title or subtitle ("Now open", "Coming soon", "Opening 2028", "Opening 2030", "Reopening soon"). The enquiry project dropdown includes Waitlist and Resale tags. Amanzoe (Porto Heli, Greece) is described by "ocean views from every room" and year-round indoor-outdoor living, and links to a villas-to-own page. — [Aman Residences](https://www.aman.com/residences)
- Greece Sotheby's shows a "Sales Track-Record" block (Riviera Bay, Voula, €3,000,000) and featured listings with name, island and price. — [Greece Sotheby's](https://www.sothebysrealty.gr/)
- Beauchamp cards show reference, price, beds, baths and surface. Rental cards show "Price on demand". — [Beauchamp](https://www.beauchamp.com/)

### Inferences
- For Kennedy's small inventory: drop the faceted search. Use 2–3 soft filters at most (destination, a price band, and "available / private / sold"), an asymmetric editorial grid of mixed card sizes, named houses, and one line of narrative per card. Keep sold properties visible with a "Sold" or "Placed privately" label as social proof.
- Lifecycle labels borrowed from branded residences ("Under offer", "Private viewing only", "Coming to market", "Off-market, by introduction") create scarcity without fake data. They also comply with the project rule against hardcoded fallbacks: show a label only when the API supplies a status.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch rendered listing cards on TMH (client-side rendering). Fantastic Frank's and Lucas Fox's listing grids weren't reachable.

## Property detail page: layout, narrative, gallery rhythm, floor plans, enquiry

### Takeaway
The documented reference is TMH: full-screen photography, architect information, a researched history section with archive imagery, and subtle animations. Branded residences put a persistent "Enquire" and a confidentiality-gated form at the centre. I couldn't verify a current detail page from any site in this session.

### Cited Findings
- TMH particulars include "information about the architects" and "a carefully researched history section, including archive imagery". Photos can be viewed in full screen. — [TMH journal](https://www.themodernhouse.com/journal/what-were-seeing-the-modern-house-relaunch)
- The Aman enquiry form collects first and last name, email, optional phone, country of residence, a project dropdown (with Waitlist and Resale), and how the visitor heard about Aman. It requires agreement to a confidentiality disclaimer. — [Aman Residences](https://www.aman.com/residences)
- Beauchamp enquiry forms carry a property reference field and a callback-time selector. — [Beauchamp](https://www.beauchamp.com/)

### Inferences
- A proposed detail-page rhythm for Kennedy's: a full-bleed title image with the house name and one evocative line; a short essay; a gallery in chapters (arrival, living, terraces, sea, night) that alternates full-bleed, pairs and detail crops; "The architecture" (architect, materials, orientation); "The place" (a destination module linking to the journal); floor plans as clean line drawings; key facts in a quiet data table; and a sticky but discreet "Arrange a private viewing" button. Where the villa is in the rental programme, add "Stay here first" and rental-yield context.

### Gaps
- No current detail page was verified for any site. Floor-plan presentation, gallery mechanics and copy length remain unconfirmed and need manual browsing.

## Destination/lifestyle content and links to listings

### Takeaway
Greece Sotheby's links destination collections to inventory (Paros Villas, Mykonos Villas, Athens Riviera Homes), but as SEO tiles rather than stories. BARNES and Aman sell the lifestyle, but the content is largely separate from specific listings. No competitor seen ties destination storytelling tightly to specific houses.

### Cited Findings
- Greece Sotheby's collection tiles are destination-based (Paros, Mykonos, Corfu, Athens Riviera, Athens Centre, Waterfront). — [Greece Sotheby's](https://www.sothebysrealty.gr/)
- The BARNES lifestyle grid and magazine sit apart from the listing modules. — [BARNES](https://www.barnes-international.com/en)
- The Aman destination mega-menu groups residences by region and country. — [Aman Residences](https://www.aman.com/residences)
- Beauchamp's editorial content is limited to a single blog teaser and practical guides (buying in France, notaries, moving to France). — [Beauchamp](https://www.beauchamp.com/)

### Inferences
- The gap is a destination page that is an editorial guide (neighbourhoods and beaches, seasonality, a reading of the architecture, Kennedy's concierge knowledge) with the live houses embedded in it. Kennedy's already has `experiences/[city]` and concierge content to reuse.

### Gaps
- Lucas Fox area guides (known for area-guide SEO) couldn't be fetched (403), so they're unverified.

## Lead capture: off-market, private viewings, newsletter, registration

### Takeaway
The leaders capture leads through valuation and status-gated routes: TMH's "How much is my home worth?", Aman's waitlist and confidentiality-gated enquiry, and the "Private" sections at Sotheby's and Beauchamp. Several Greek sites rely mainly on phone, WhatsApp and generic buyer and seller buttons.

### Cited Findings
- TMH: "How much is my home worth?", "Get a free market appraisal" and "Open Home" events. No newsletter CTA was visible on the homepage. — [The Modern House](https://www.themodernhouse.com/)
- Aman: a header "Enquire" button, "Make an enquiry", a "Get inspired" sign-up, and Waitlist/Resale options. — [Aman Residences](https://www.aman.com/residences)
- Greece Sotheby's has a "Private" nav item. No email signup was seen on the homepage. It relies on phone, email and a WhatsApp button. — [Greece Sotheby's](https://www.sothebysrealty.gr/)
- Beauchamp keeps an off-market "Private Collection" for Mykonos, Athens, Syros and Paros, and runs a monthly newsletter. — [Beauchamp search result](https://www.beauchamp.com/?p=19575); [Beauchamp](https://www.beauchamp.com/)
- BARNES: a newsletter, property alerts (at least 3 criteria required), an account, and "Request an estimate". — [BARNES](https://www.barnes-international.com/en)
- Ploumis: "I WANT TO BUY - RENT" and "I WANT TO SELL - RENT" buttons, a "Private Sales" menu item, and a newsletter ("Never Miss a Moment of Luxury"). — [Ploumis Sotiropoulos](https://www.ploumis-sotiropoulos.gr/en)

### Inferences
- Kennedy's could offer a "Private Register" with a short brief (destination, budget band, timing, rent or own), which unlocks off-market houses and invitations to private viewings. Pair it with a quarterly "Letter from the Cyclades" newsletter that carries journal stories rather than listing blasts. The sell side could be "Entrust your home", with valuation tied to rental-yield data from the management business.

### Gaps
- No conversion data for any of these patterns was found.

## Animations, transitions, micro-interactions, mobile

### Takeaway
Little reliable evidence surfaced. The only documented principle is TMH's restraint: "subtle, short animations… in selected sections".

### Cited Findings
- TMH: "Subtle, short animations are used in selected sections to accentuate the visual content." — [TMH journal](https://www.themodernhouse.com/journal/what-were-seeing-the-modern-house-relaunch)
- Beauchamp uses a sticky header on scroll. — [Beauchamp](https://www.beauchamp.com/)
- An Aman digital case study cites performance problems on its previous Drupal site, dedicated Residences landing pages, and multilingual support. — [CTI Digital](https://www.ctidigital.com/work/aman-case-study)

### Inferences
- Restraint reads as premium: slow image fades and reveals, unhurried crossfades between gallery chapters, cursor-following captions on desktop, and no carousels autoplaying text. Performance matters too, since Aman's re-platform was driven by speed.

### Gaps
- I found no Awwwards or SiteInspire entries or agency case studies covering motion on these sites. A browser-based visual and motion audit is needed.

## What feels premium vs dated; ideas for Kennedy's; competitor weaknesses

### Takeaway
Premium comes from curation, editorial subject matter, named houses, a consistent type system and visible track record. Dated comes from search-first homepages, mixed sales and rental and congress offers, stats bands, FAQ walls, SEO footers, placeholder links and luxury adjectives in place of content. Mediterranean competitors (Ploumis, Beauchamp Riviera, partly Greece Sotheby's and BARNES) mostly sit in the second group. Nobody combines rental-stay experience with sales storytelling.

### Cited Findings
- Signs of a dated, brochure-style build at Ploumis: text-heavy blocks, a dense filter panel, duplicated nav, "#" placeholder links, inconsistent naming, and off-site press links. — [Ploumis Sotiropoulos](https://www.ploumis-sotiropoulos.gr/en)
- Beauchamp mixes sales, holiday rentals and Congress rentals, and gives conflicting head-office statements (London vs Cannes). — [Beauchamp](https://www.beauchamp.com/)
- BARNES is inconsistent on its country count, and its footer is an SEO directory. — [BARNES](https://www.barnes-international.com/en)
- Greece Sotheby's is premium in tone but leans on adjectives, and it has no email capture on its homepage. — [Greece Sotheby's](https://www.sothebysrealty.gr/)
- TMH is credited by the press as "an estate agency like no other". — [The Modern House](https://www.themodernhouse.com/)

### Inferences
Ideas to adopt (the gap to exploit):
1. Make the sales section a publication ("Kennedy's Houses" or a journal-led landing page) with named houses, an essay per house, and an architect and materials module.
2. Present collections, not search, and keep "Recently placed" visible.
3. Use branded-residence lifecycle labels plus a private register for off-market houses.
4. Bridge rental and sales: "Stay first, own later", "Kennedy's-managed after purchase" and rental-income context. This is a structural advantage competitors lack.
5. Write destination guides with embedded houses, and use Kennedy's concierge knowledge as editorial authority.
6. Keep one disciplined type pairing (serif + grotesk), a mineral palette, generous whitespace and slow motion.
7. Show press and track record as quiet quotes, not stat bands.

### Gaps
- No visual or competitive audit of Mykonos/Paros boutique agencies was completed. The search surfaced only aggregators (luxuryestate.com) and small agencies (Greek Exclusive Properties, Ktimatoemporiki), and none was confirmed as design-led. Kretz/Coldwell Banker Côte d'Azur and Marbella boutiques weren't researched. I found no Awwwards or SiteInspire entries or 2025–2026 design-press coverage for these sites.
